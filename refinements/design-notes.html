More careful decisions.

The brief was to refine Ink After Dark in small, considered steps. These six studies keep the same content, card anatomy, workshop photograph, crop, and opened-event structure. Their differences come from type, optical sizing, spacing, and tightly related dark palettes.

What stays constant

Every list has the same three events, with only category, title, venue, date, time, and price on the cards. The same Ink After Dark photo fills the opened frame. The longer description, inclusions, schedule, arrival guidance, and practical details are available by scrolling or following the detail link.

The frames are live HTML and CSS. Bookmark marks and the Saved label are visual references in this static study; they do not simulate saving. Ink After Dark opens through an ordinary HTML link, and the detail links scroll to the longer listing. No application framework, browser JavaScript, API, account, or checkout is involved.

The spacing and type system

The shared spacing scale is 4, 8, 12, 16, 20, 24, 28, 32, 40, and 48px. Small gaps group information; larger gaps separate decisions. The same horizontal inset aligns event names, venue lines, facts, price, and action. The scale is an internal consistency choice, not a universal rule for good design.

Type is optically normalized instead of assigning the same nominal size to every font. Source Sans 3 is slightly larger to balance its smaller x-height; Newsreader’s serif titles have more nominal size and a lighter weight. The other sans families stay near the original scale. Body copy uses a 24px reading line: 14px/24px for the main sans treatments and 16px/24px for Source Sans. Event title lines are 32px, and detail title lines are 40px or 44px.

Variable fonts provide real intermediate weights without synthetic bolding. Expanded tracking is limited to the wordmark and category labels; event titles use restrained optical negative tracking. Running text keeps its natural spacing. Dates, times, and prices use lining, tabular numerals for a calmer scan. The year and duration are separated by tone rather than unnecessary changes of size, so the information sits on a common baseline.

Dark color, without muddy hierarchy

Each palette is built around a near-neutral dark background, a slightly raised card surface, off-white primary text, a distinct secondary text value, and one muted warm accent. Hue varies subtly; lightness does the main work of organizing the content. The accent is reserved for price, the primary action, and a small category cue.

The checked title, secondary, category, accent, action, and first-photo-text combinations all exceed 4.5:1; the lowest of these checks is 6.10:1. The photo check conservatively assumes a white photographic pixel behind the first text line. The actual selected image is darker there. These are targeted contrast checks, not a claim of full accessibility certification. The W3C contrast guidance explains the 4.5:1 text threshold.

A functional scrim makes the lower photo a reliable reading surface while preserving the activity in the upper half. Its strength increases before the first small text. This avoids depending on a perfectly dark patch in a submitted photo. The quiet dot texture is the same existing site-owned asset, used at low opacity.

Small details that make the system hold together

  • The first-photo text groups sit on explicit line boxes, avoiding the fractional vertical rhythm caused by an inline eyebrow.
  • Every primary action is at least 48px high. Close controls and detail links have at least 44px targets. The Ink After Dark card has a full-card HTML link.
  • There is one icon family throughout: Radix. Glyphs are local SVG assets exported from the library, with the original license included.
  • Controls, title, facts, and price retain the same order in every version.
  • The mobile screens scroll without reserving a desktop scrollbar, so both frames remain equal in width.
  • At narrow widths, text can wrap; content is not truncated to preserve a fixed card height.
  • Reduced-motion preferences remove smooth anchor scrolling. A higher-contrast preference strengthens secondary text and removes the background texture.
  • All fonts and images are local. There are no third-party requests when viewing the deliverables. Fontsource’s variable font documentation describes the bundled font approach.

My read

01 Soft Focus is the closest balanced evolution of the original. 06 Quiet Air gives the same idea more composure through space and a lighter typographic touch. 03 Warm Editorial is the most useful contrast if you want to decide whether a restrained serif belongs in Burque’s voice.

Start with those at full phone size, then compare 02, 04, and 05 for reading comfort and character. None changes the underlying product idea.
